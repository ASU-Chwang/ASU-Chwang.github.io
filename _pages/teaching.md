# Teaching & Mentoring

My teaching experience spans **secondary-school mathematics education, private tutoring, university-level research supervision, and science outreach activities**.

Across these activities, I aim to adapt explanations to the learner's background and level of understanding, break complex concepts into manageable steps, and encourage continuous and independent learning.

---

## Teaching Philosophy

I believe that effective teaching begins with understanding **where the learner currently stands**.

Rather than presenting knowledge as a finished result, I try to guide students through a step-by-step learning process:

* identifying difficulties and gaps in understanding;
* explaining complex concepts using simple language and concrete examples;
* progressing from fundamental problems to more challenging tasks;
* encouraging students to explain their own reasoning;
* providing regular feedback and adjusting the learning plan when necessary.

This approach has influenced both my classroom teaching and my supervision of university students engaged in research.

## High School Mathematics Teaching

**Qiqihar Experimental High School, China**
*Teaching Practicum | Mathematics | One semester | Approx. 60 students*

During my undergraduate studies at Qiqihar University, I completed a one-semester teaching practicum at Qiqihar Experimental High School.

I was primarily responsible for mathematics classes for approximately **60 second-year high school students**, teaching one class per week.

My responsibilities included:

* preparing lesson plans and teaching materials;
* delivering mathematics lectures;
* designing examples and exercises appropriate to students' levels;
* communicating regularly with students;
* identifying individual learning difficulties;
* improving teaching methods based on student feedback and classroom performance.

I placed particular emphasis on organizing mathematical concepts in a logical sequence and using familiar examples to make abstract concepts easier to understand.

## Private Mathematics Tutoring

**High School Mathematics Tutor**
*Approximately 2.5 years | 3 students per group | One session per day*

I provided small-group mathematics tutoring for high school students for approximately two and a half years.

For each group, I first assessed the students' strengths, weaknesses, and learning pace. Based on this assessment, I developed both **monthly short-term plans** and **semester-level learning plans**.

My teaching approach emphasized progression from:

> **Fundamental concepts → Basic exercises → Applied problems → University entrance examination-level problems**

Rather than focusing only on obtaining the correct answer, I encouraged students to understand the reasoning behind each solution process.

The effectiveness of this individualized approach was positively evaluated by students and their parents, and several new tutoring opportunities were introduced through their recommendations.

## Continuous Improvement of the Course

Through repeated implementation of the program, I continuously reviewed and refined the course design.

One of the main challenges in the early sessions was **time management**. Some activities required more time than expected, especially data distribution, manual correction, and result collection.

By adjusting the pacing of the lectures, simplifying explanations, and improving the workflow of the practical sessions, I was able to develop a more balanced course structure and improve the overall learning experience.

Based on these experiences, I focused particularly on three areas of improvement.

### 1. Automating Data Transfer and Collection

In the original workflow, distributing datasets for AI processing and collecting manually corrected results required substantial time.

To improve this process, I developed an **automated data distribution and collection system**.

This reduced the required operation time from approximately:

> **30 minutes → 10 minutes**

The saved time could then be used for explanation, discussion, and hands-on learning.

### 2. Improving Comparative Visualization

To help participants compare multiple processing results more intuitively, I improved the visualization method by assigning **different colors to different anatomical structures and processing results**.

This allowed students to identify differences between results more easily and made the comparison process more visual and engaging.

The change was especially effective for participants who had little prior experience with medical image analysis.

### 3. Increasing Engagement through Personalized Organ Models

From previous sessions, I found that some students were initially less interested in the bronchus as a learning topic.

To increase engagement, I introduced a final activity called:

> **“Create Your Own Personalized Organ Model”**

Participants could customize their bronchial model and create a unique 3D representation as a small souvenir of the course.

This activity helped connect the technical content with a more creative and personal experience.

As a result, participants became more actively involved in the course, and the activity received positive feedback.

## Educational Reflection

These repeated teaching experiences have helped me recognize that effective science education requires not only clear explanations, but also careful consideration of:

* lesson pacing;
* workflow design;
* visualization;
* student engagement;
* hands-on interaction;
* continuous improvement based on previous experience.

By combining technical development with educational design, I have tried to make advanced topics such as AI and medical image processing more accessible and enjoyable for younger learners.

The experience has also strengthened my interest in developing educational activities that connect **research, engineering, visualization, and hands-on learning**.
